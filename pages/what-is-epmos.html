EXPLANATION

The distinction that matters

An intelligent system may be technically able to perform an action without having permission to perform it. EPMOS treats that distinction as a first-order design concern. A good answer, a reliable tool call, or a successful prior task does not grant permanent authority.

Why EPMOS exists

AI systems can reason, write, research, code, access tools, handle records, coordinate workflows, and initiate external actions. As capability grows, the important questions become: Who authorized this? Under what conditions? What evidence supports it? What happens when evidence is missing or the system is wrong? Can the action be reconstructed and can authority be revoked?

Public-safe model

EPMOS describes the requirements and reasons for governed behavior without publishing protected implementation details. Authority should be scoped, granted, enforceable, conditional, and revocable. Consequential actions require stronger controls than ordinary explanation, and uncertainty should remain visible rather than being converted into confidence.
